\section{Information decomposition and all boundary cases}
For fixed observable masses, every completion has the joint table
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{c|cccc} &1&\cdots&K&\infty\\\hline
 L=1&a_1&\cdots&a_K&u\\
 L=0&b_1&\cdots&b_K&c-u
 \end{array}.
\end{equation}
Its entries are nonnegative and sum to one exactly when $0\le u\le c$. The row harmful marginal is $A+u$. Conditioning on columns gives
\begin{equation}
 H(L\mid J_\Pi)=\sum_{k:a_k+b_k>0}(a_k+b_k)h\left(\frac{a_k}{a_k+b_k}\right)+q(u,c-u).
\end{equation}
This proves the information profile in the article without a selection-model assumption. When $r,c>0$, put $p_R=A/r$, $p_U=u/c$ and $\theta=rp_R+cp_U$. Then
\begin{align}
 \Sprof_\Pi(u)-\tau_\Pi
 &=h(\theta)-r h(p_R)-c h(p_U)\\
 &=r\KL\{\operatorname{Bern}(p_R)\Vert\operatorname{Bern}(\theta)\}
 +c\KL\{\operatorname{Bern}(p_U)\Vert\operatorname{Bern}(\theta)\}\ge0.\label{eq:js}
\end{align}
The equality is interpreted by continuity if a marginal is zero. For $0<r,c<1$, equality in the divergence inequality holds precisely at $p_R=p_U$, proving $u=Ac/r$. Thus $\tau_\Pi$ is the minimal possible full information and is determined by the resolved timing distribution. Equation~\eqref{eq:js} also displays the model as an information budget for terminal selection after accounting for observed timing.

For interior hidden masses, $h'(x)=\log\{(1-x)/x\}$ and $h''(x)=-1/\{x(1-x)\}$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \Sprof''(u)=\frac1u+\frac1{c-u}-\frac1{A+u}-\frac1{G+c-u}
 =\frac{A}{u(A+u)}+\frac{G}{(c-u)(G+c-u)}.
\end{equation}
If $r>0$, at least one numerator is positive, so the profile is strictly convex whenever $c>0$. If $A=0<G$, the derivative is $\log\{(G+c-u)/(c-u)\}>0$ and the minimum is $u=0$. If $G=0<A$, the derivative is $\log\{u/(A+u)\}<0$ and the minimum is $u=c$. If $c=0$, the only completion is $u=0$, with information $h(A)-T_\Pi=\tau_\Pi$. If $r=0$, $c=1$, the profile is $h(u)-h(u)=0$ throughout $[0,1]$. These cases also show why no minimum-information risk is defined from resolved cases when $r=0$.

For $r>0,c>0$, strict convexity and continuity imply that the sublevel set is empty below its minimum, is the unique minimizer at equality, and is a nonempty compact interval above it. It can touch either boundary; an interior root need not exist on both branches. Since every feasible completion is a valid table and no other completion is possible, its risk interval is sharp. If only coarse observations are supplied, any completion can be lifted to a hypothetical finer representation with no additional within-coarse-band outcome dependence, preserving mutual information. Known fine margins restrict such a lift, which is why the article states sharpness with respect to the supplied partition.

\section{Refinement, strictness and decision frontiers}
Let each finite coarse band $B$ collect fine bands with masses $m_k=a_k+b_k$ and harmful proportions $r_k=a_k/m_k$. For $m_B=\sum_{k\in B}m_k>0$, its harmful proportion is $\bar r_B=\sum_{k\in B}m_kr_k/m_B$. Its entropy gain is
\begin{equation}
 m_Bh(\bar r_B)-\sum_{k\in B}m_kh(r_k)\ge0.
\end{equation}
Strict concavity makes the inequality strict exactly when positive-mass proportions within $B$ differ. Sum over coarse bands. The unresolved column is unchanged, so this sum is $T_c-T_f$, independent of its hidden label split. The chain rule for the deterministic map $J_c=g(J_f)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 I(L;J_f)=I(L;J_c)+I(L;J_f\mid J_c),
\end{equation}
and identifies the sum with the conditional information. Comparing profiles gives nesting at every common budget, including empty-set cases.

For the sufficient strictness condition in the article, fine compatibility gives $\rho\ge\tau_f>\tau_c$. Therefore the coarse right endpoint is strictly to the right of its minimizer. If it is below $c$, its coarse profile value is $\rho$ and its fine profile value is $\rho+\Delta$. Fine feasibility excludes that point. Compactness prevents the fine upper endpoint from approaching it without attaining it, proving strict inequality. If both right endpoints are $c$, the upper risks coincide despite $\Delta>0$. Thus informative timing is insufficient for strict improvement when both bounds are saturated. If $c=0$, there is no hidden risk to tighten.

For the frontier, first note that every compatible set contains $\theta^\dagger=A/r$. Hence $\beta<\theta^\dagger$ is intrinsically uncertifiable even if $A\le\beta$; $\beta<A$ is the stronger direct obstruction. If $\beta\ge A+c$, every completion is safe. Otherwise, $\theta^\dagger\le\beta<A+c$ puts $u_\beta=\beta-A$ on the right branch, where the profile is strictly increasing beyond its minimum. If $\rho\le\Sprof(u_\beta)$, its sublevel-set right endpoint is at most $u_\beta$. If $\rho>\Sprof(u_\beta)$, continuity gives a feasible point to the right of $u_\beta$ (as $u_\beta<c$), so certification fails. Equality therefore belongs to the certifying side. Subtracting the two profiles at $u_\beta$ gives the exact frontier shift. In the case $\beta=\theta^\dagger<A+c$, the only certifying budget is $\rho=\tau$.

\section{Proof of the simultaneous and projected guarantee}
For a true category probability $p_j\in(0,1)$, write $X_{j,i}=\ind\{Y_i=j\}$. For any fixed $z\in[0,1]$, the process
\begin{equation}
 Z_n(z;p_j)=\prod_{i=1}^n
 \left(\frac z{p_j}\right)^{X_{j,i}}
 \left(\frac{1-z}{1-p_j}\right)^{1-X_{j,i}}
\end{equation}
is a nonnegative martingale, since its next multiplicative factor has conditional expectation $p_j(z/p_j)+(1-p_j)\{(1-z)/(1-p_j)\}=1$. Integrating against a Beta$(1/2,1/2)$ law preserves this property by conditional Tonelli, with initial value one, and gives the article's $M_{j,n}(p_j)$. At $p_j=0$, all $X_{j,i}=0$ almost surely and the limiting mixture is nonincreasing with initial value one; $p_j=1$ is analogous. The maximal inequality therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \Pbb\{\exists n:M_{j,n}(p_j)\ge d/\delta\}\le\delta/d.
\end{equation}
A union bound over the $d$ categories proves simultaneous containment in every raw interval with probability at least $1-\delta$. Taking running intersections and intersecting with the simplex preserve this same event. Categories within an event need not be independent. No union bound over monitoring times is used.

On simultaneous containment, the true summary belongs to the exact observable-law region at every $n$. The actual terminal harmful mass supplies a feasible completion because its finest-path information is at most $\rho$, and coarse information cannot be larger. Therefore $\theta$ is contained in the union of partition-specific risk sets at every $n$. Its supremum is bounded by the aggregate relaxation and by any conservative numerical enclosure. This proves the article's time-uniform risk guarantee. Restricting reports to checkpoints or adding evidence gates can only remove opportunities to certify, so neither requires extra error spending. If the sensitivity assumption fails, this feasibility step fails; compatibility of some other completion does not repair it.

The event also implies simultaneous coverage of every member of the partition-specific identified set: on it, the entire true observable-law set is part of each projection. Thus the guarantee is stronger than pointwise containment of a selected terminal completion, while the implemented relaxation may be wider than necessary. Across multiple partitions or multiple streams the same reasoning needs a simultaneous observable region or an explicit union allocation; the single-partition theorem does not supply such an allocation automatically.

\section{Envelope and branch-and-bound derivation}
For any selected set of categories $B$, simplex membership implies
\begin{equation}
 \max\{\sum_{j\in B}\ell_j,1-\sum_{j\notin B}v_j,0\}
 \le\sum_{j\in B}p_j\le
 \min\{\sum_{j\in B}v_j,1-\sum_{j\notin B}\ell_j,1\}.
\end{equation}
These inequalities give the aggregate mass bounds. For $a,b>0$, $\partial q/\partial a=\log\{(a+b)/a\}\ge0$ and $\partial q/\partial b=\log\{(a+b)/b\}\ge0$. Continuity extends monotonicity to boundaries. Thus evaluating $q$ at simultaneous lower or upper coordinates gives valid lower or upper entropy bounds. The raw upper sum is valid but can be loose; intersecting it with the resolved-mass entropy ceiling and $\log2$ tightens it. A ceiling is not needed to turn the raw sum into an upper bound. This monotonicity argument, rather than attainability of simultaneous upper coordinates, justifies the envelope.

Every categorical summary maps into $\Eset_n$. Since $\Sprof=h(A+u)-T-q(u,c-u)$ decreases in $T$, replacing the actual $T$ by $T_U$ can only enlarge its feasible set. For a search box $B=[A_-,A_+]\times[G_-,G_+]\times[u_-,u_+]$, define
\begin{align}
 c_-&=\max(c_L,1-A_+-G_+,0),&c_+&=\min(c_U,1-A_--G_-,1),\\
 \tilde u_+&=\min(u_+,c_+),&
 z_-&=\max(0,c_--\tilde u_+),\quad z_+=\max(0,c_+-u_-).
\end{align}
Impossible mass constraints discard the box. Otherwise every feasible point has $u\in[u_-,\tilde u_+]$, terminal beneficial mass in $[z_-,z_+]$, and $\theta\in[A_-+u_-,\min(1,A_++\tilde u_+)]$. A lower enclosure is
\begin{equation}
 \underline S_B=\max\left\{0,\ \inf_{x\in[A_-+u_-,\min(1,A_++\tilde u_+)]}h(x)
       -T_U-\sup_{u\in[u_-,\tilde u_+],\,z\in[z_-,z_+]}q(u,z)-\eta_B\right\},
\end{equation}
Here $\eta_B=0$ for the revised implementation: every displayed arithmetic bound is evaluated in the enclosing direction rather than corrected by an empirical allowance. The archived implementation instead used $32(K+1)\operatorname{ulp}(1)$ for specified floating operations. The binary-entropy infimum is at an interval endpoint. The mass entropy $q$ is increasing in each argument and is continuous at zero; lower corners therefore give lower bounds. Its upper bound is the smaller of a direct Arb interval upper bound, when both arguments are bounded away from zero, and $(u_++z_+)\log 2$. Taking the maximum with zero need not bound a negative relaxed expression from below. It nevertheless preserves every feasible relaxed point when $\rho\ge0$, since replacing a negative lower bound by zero cannot make it exceed $\rho$. Thus a box with $\underline S_B>\rho$ contains neither a feasible point of $V_n$ nor an original compatible completion.

The following argument covers the revised functions in \texttt{confidence.py}, \texttt{envelope.py}, \texttt{projection.py} and \texttt{certification.py}. Its arithmetic assumptions are Python's exact integer/rational operations, correctly rounded binary64 conversion and division, adjacent-float stepping, and the enclosure contract of Arb (Johansson, 2017, in the article reference list). Supplied binary64 endpoints and budgets denote their exact dyadic values. Endpoints are finite and lie in $[0,1]$, tolerances are finite and positive, and each process executes the numerical calls serially without external mutation of the Arb context. The repository uses separate processes for parallel streams. The argument is a source-level numerical proof under these runtime contracts, not a machine-checked proof of the language, libraries or hardware.

\emph{Directed arithmetic and confidence input.} For an exact rational $v$, the code first converts to a nearest binary64 value $f$, then compares $f$ with $v$ exactly. A lower conversion moves to the preceding float only when $f>v$; the upper conversion is symmetric. Exact rational sums followed by these conversions enclose all mass sums, complements, objectives and reported gaps. An Arb lower or upper endpoint is first extracted as an exact integer times a power of two and then converted in the same direction. Nonfinite Arb results give infinite bounds or a numerical failure, never a favorable sign.
For each category, the beta-mixture log likelihood minus $\log(d/\delta)$ is evaluated as an Arb ball, including the beta-function term and threshold. This function is convex in the probability. Inversion maintains an outer boundary point and a certified nonpositive inner point on each side; a positive interval sign moves the outer point, and a nonpositive interval sign moves the inner point. An unresolved sign leaves the outer bracket in place. If no inner sign is certified, the entire corresponding side $[0,\widehat p]$ or $[\widehat p,1]$ is retained. Hence the returned interval contains the exact mixture sublevel set even when inversion stops early. The zero-count and all-count boundaries use their continuous likelihood values. Running intersections use endpoint maxima and minima and preserve the simultaneous-containment event; an empty intersection raises a violation and supplies no certificate.

\emph{Summary representation.} A category-subset sum is enclosed both directly and by its simplex complement. The larger lower endpoint and smaller upper endpoint still enclose every law in the confidence rectangle intersected with the simplex. Monotonicity of $q$ supplies the resolved-entropy bounds, additionally capped above by the resolved-mass upper bound times $\log2$. Point category tables also use directed aggregate sums and an Arb entropy interval. Their ``singleton'' flag describes the categories, not exact real entropy. Approximate input normalization accepted by the population data layer is never taken as an exact simplex identity by the certifier.

\emph{Upper-search invariant and exits.} Initially, every point of $V_n$ is in the aggregate box. Directed geometry tests reject only impossible mass constraints, and the preceding information lower bound rejects only points outside the information budget. A split uses a representable midpoint inside its parent interval; its two closed children cover the parent, even when rounding makes one child identical to it. A retained box contributes the upward-rounded bound $\min(1,A_++u_+)$. Consequently the largest active or queued objective upper bound encloses the remaining supremum. Incumbents can raise this reported maximum but cannot lower it. Objective-gap, decision, box-width, stall and node-cap exits retain the relevant active and queued bounds. If arithmetic fails during a step, the active parent is retained even if only some children have been inserted. Failure before a conservative result is available propagates as a technical failure. When no boxes or incumbent remain, the reported upper bound is one. Thus convergence is unnecessary for domination of $V_n$.

\emph{Singleton shortcut.} The scalar right-branch search is used only for point category inputs with point aggregate mass intervals. It additionally checks exact equality of those aggregates with the category sums and exact normalization with $c$. A failed identity check gives an assumption-free upper bound, without an incumbent. Exactly normalized tables with non-point aggregate sums use the box search; non-normalized point tables go directly to the conservative fallback. For fixed valid $A,G,c$, the largest relaxed endpoint uses exactly the supplied entropy upper endpoint $T_U$, not an independently recomputed nominal entropy. The initial right-branch lower bracket is at or above the exact minimizer $Ac/(A+G)$, and the upper bracket is $c$. Only a certified profile value above $\rho$ can reduce the upper bracket. Convexity on this branch proves that every feasible hidden mass remains below it; an ambiguous sign cannot reduce that upper bracket. Upward conversion of $A+u_{\rm hi}$ gives the risk enclosure. Zero resolved or unresolved mass is covered by the continuous boundary formulas. The label \texttt{EXACT\_SINGLETON} is used only when a verified lower incumbent equals the upper bound; otherwise the result is enclosed or a conservative fallback.

\emph{Feasible witnesses and gaps.} Candidate allocation and the population root solver are proposal mechanisms only. For a proposed table, the certifier sums its supplied category entries exactly and defines terminal mass as the rational complement $c=1-A-G$. It checks exact category, aggregate, terminal and hidden-mass membership, including $0\le u\le c$. The actual resolved entropy must lie inside its envelope, and an Arb upper bound on the actual table's information must be at most $\rho$. These checks construct a normalized original joint table regardless of the proposal's rounded aggregate metadata. Its risk is converted downward before becoming an incumbent. The singleton path applies the same entropy-membership and actual-information conditions after its exact mass identities. Failed or unresolved checks give no witness. Thus an incumbent cannot exceed the original supremum and also belongs below the relaxed supremum. The reported gap is rounded upward from the enclosure minus this lower incumbent; no witness means no claimed gap. A lower incumbent above $\beta$ refutes certification by this projection, without claiming that the true stream risk exceeds $\beta$.

\emph{Rejection searches and final assessment.} The compatibility search assigns each box the downward bound $\max\{0,\inf q(A,G)-T_U\}$, which is below every original compatibility floor in that box. The intrinsic search uses a downward enclosure of $A_-/(A_-+G_+)$, taking zero if its denominator vanishes, and discards boxes only by the same safe geometry and information tests. This ratio bounds $A/(A+G)$ for every compatible original law retained in the box. In both searches, the minimum active or queued lower bound is therefore a global lower bound. Approximate point values can trigger stopping but never prune boxes or become the reported lower bound. At any early return the active box is included; after splitting its covering children are included. An empty retained queue returns zero rather than a point estimate. Exceptions propagate without a scientific result. If zero resolved mass remains plausible, the intrinsic bound is withheld. Finally, assessment checks that the projection and requested sensitivity budget agree. Rejections use strict comparisons with upward-rounded budget-plus-guard thresholds, while certification uses only $\widehat U_n\le\beta$ after the evidence gates. These facts compose the confidence input, summary enclosure, searches and assessment into the numerical condition of the article's time-uniform theorem under the stated assumptions.

The former population-midpoint shortcut and the archived floating wrappers are not covered retrospectively by this proof. Independent high-precision directional regressions check the repaired boundaries, entropy metadata, likelihood signs and search exits; passing those tests is supporting implementation evidence, not the enclosure argument itself. Historical experiment records are preserved without claiming that all their saved decisions were reverified under the revised code.

Compatibility rejection uses a lower bound on $q(A,G)-T_U$, rounded down and truncated at zero. Intrinsic rejection uses a lower bound on $A/(A+G)$ over compatible-plausible boxes. For example, on a box with $A_-+G_+>0$, $A_-/(A_-+G_+)$ is a valid lower bound. If zero resolved mass is plausible, intrinsic rejection is withheld. Strict comparison guards are $10^{-12}$. Population root tolerance is $10^{-12}$; identity residual tolerance is $10^{-10}$; categorical inversion tolerance is $10^{-12}$; the nominal outer objective gap is $10^{-6}$ with a 2,000,000-node cap. A normalized box-width exit or stall is reported separately from objective convergence.
