\title{TrajCert: Sharp risk identification and anytime-valid certification with informative outcome-resolution paths}
\author{\draftadmin{AUTHOR NAMES REQUIRED}}
\address{\draftadmin{AFFILIATIONS AND CORRESPONDING AUTHOR REQUIRED}}
\begin{abstract}
Outcome verification can depend on an unobserved harmful outcome, both through resolution timing and through nonresolution at a terminal horizon. Collapsing these paths to a binary verification indicator can discard information needed for risk certification. We study binary harmful risk under a fixed mutual-information budget between the latent outcome and a prespecified categorical resolution path. The compatible risk set is a sharp interval determined by a convex one-dimensional information profile. Its minimum is observable, giving an exact compatibility floor. Deterministic refinement shifts this profile by an observable conditional-information increment, characterizing when timing changes a certification decision. A simultaneous categorical confidence sequence, combined with conservative numerical projection, gives a time-uniform upper-risk certificate under a stable observable law. In a frozen synthetic study with 100 independent streams per cell, a two-band analysis certified all streams by 5,000 matured events under strong informative timing, whereas endpoint-only analysis certified 96 by 50,000 events. At a stricter risk budget, the endpoint population bound precluded certification, while refined analyses certified 99--100 streams by 50,000 events. A matched control demonstrates the finite-sample cost of resolution without timing information. These results identify when retaining verification paths improves both population decisions and finite-sample certification.
\end{abstract}
