\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro} 
Risk certificates should account for events with both resolved and yet-unresolved adverse status. If adverse outcomes are less frequent to resolve, the pool of resolved observations may not reflect the wider population, and even within this pool the elapsed time until resolution can be correlated with adversity. A binary resolution flag removes the latter. 

Accordingly, this work addresses a refined version of whether the resolution time carries information about harm. Under a stated degree of allowed dependency, can the resolution information support risk certification beyond that afforded by the endpoint distribution alone? The interplay between the resulting identification problem, a choice of sensitivity parameter and sampling variability is analysed. Missing labels imply some level of identification uncertainty irrespective of sample size. A sensitivity parameter defines a class of possible resolutions of the missing labels, and sampling variability determines whether certification is feasible at a prescribed risk bound prior to study termination.

An adverse event of interest $L\in\{0,1\}$ is modelled along with a discretised resolution trajectory up to $J^*$, where the final category absorbs all non-resolution events. The estimand is $\theta = \Pbb(L = 1)$, with the observed resolution trajectory providing incomplete information about it. The (unknown) amount of information revealed by the trajectory is specified via $I(L; J^*) \leq \rho$, for $\rho$ an information budget in units of nats. $\rho$ simultaneously constrains any information that is correlated with the terminal non-resolution state. It is not interpreted as missing-at-random or estimated from the streaming (incompletely labelled) dataset. Discretising time-to-event information into a deterministic mapping yields a coarser trajectory with the same $\rho$ budget.

For this setup, the information profile of the remaining free mass corresponding to terminal adverse events is characterised. The profile is a strictly convex function of that mass. The minimal information equals the information provided by the observed trajectory times the probability of being resolved, and inverting the profile recovers the sharpest bounds on $\theta$ at a given $\rho$. Successive refinements shift the information profile upwards by the observable conditional information $\Delta$, a known quantity independent of the residual free mass. This relationship is exploited to obtain tight criteria for parameter compatibility with $\rho$, and for certifying $\theta \leq r$ against a specified risk budget $r$. This work quantifies how these criteria vary with the resolution trajectory and illustrates the method by constructing confidence sequences on the resolved outcomes via numerical optimisation (with a suitably conservative envelope on the optimum to maintain valid coverage).

Applying this to a synthetic population example, an endpoint-only risk bound of $0.065835$ improves to $0.055241$ when observing the timing of event resolution ($|\mathcal{J}|=8$ categories), falling below a hypothetical risk budget of $6\%$. In a simulation study with pre-specified discretisation, the information content of the resolved trajectory enabled certification of 100/100 samples before 5k matured events, versus only 96/100 below 50k matured events using endpoint labels. This advantage was shown to vanish under an analogous trajectory without the timing information, establishing a connection to the availability of timing signal.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related} 
\paragraph{Data with unobserved values} 
In the setting of data with unobserved outcomes, this model builds upon partial-identification results which require characterisation over the space of compatible distributions \citep{manski2005}. Using sensitivity parameters, or otherwise accounting for additional restrictions in the missing-data mechanism, avoids reliance on the case-complete estimate as a surrogate for the total population risk \citep{carpenter2021}. Missing-pattern mixtures and related parameterisations for modelling missing categorical data are introduced in \citep{little1993}; see also the sensitivity analysis of \citet{shardell2009} to the extent to which the timing of partial observations is associated with event outcomes. The most closely related line of work treats the data-generation scheme of observations and non-observations as information, with e.g.\ \citep{dalygrafstein2026} using this perspective in Bayesian analyses of missing categorically-valued outcomes, and imposing further auxiliary structure on the likelihood for posterior estimation. TrajCert instead uses an agnostic restriction on the mutual information shared between the outcome and its observation pathway.

Multiple aspects of the data-generating process can yield further information about the unobserved population parameter. For example, having several forms of missingness allows \citet{jiang2018} to impose missingness at-random for a subset of the missingness types; alternatively, \citet{miao2025} use sequential callback data and assume stableness-of-resistance between callbacks to achieve point identification. TrajCert uses a discretised resolved time-course in conjunction with an unlabelled residual; in neither sense assumes resistance equivalence or specifies a missing-at-random mechanism, and admits some terminal non-label completions. TrajCert makes use of the information in resolved timing to quantify this advantage vs.\ using the resolved endpoint alone.

\paragraph{Alternative sensitivity specifications} 
As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:model}, the parameterisation of the amount of uncertainty introduced by the sensitivity assumption leads to distinct classes of valid population laws. \citet{kline2013} propose an assumption bounding the maximum Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance of the marginal distribution of $L$ given resolved or non-resolved data. Divergence measures bounding the space of underlying distributions are proposed in \citep{christensen2023}, while \citet{jin2026} similarly introduce $f$-sensitivity models which bound mean sensitivity due to unobserved confounding, and \citet{jung2026} derive information-theoretic causal bounds using a propensity-based divergence restriction. These approaches produce bounds on different quantities, and in particular do not suggest data-driven estimates of the $J^*$-dependent sensitivity measure adopted here. This measure corresponds to the Shannon information between the endpoint label $L$ and the (incompletely observed) resolution path, with the associated independent distribution varying with the unknown marginal law of $L$. These methods target causal questions on data collected conditionally on auxiliary covariates, whereas the observed distribution has only one missing degree of freedom. The main results characterise this distribution and its associated parameter space at any specified sensitivity level. Further, the technique of information anchoring is applied to sensitivity estimation in multiple-imputation models \citep{cro2019}, albeit with a different meaning of information than the dependence considered here.

\paragraph{Partially-identified and time-varying inference} 
It is well-established that the interpretation of confidence regions enclosing a (non-point) identification region, and of confidence intervals over components thereof, differ \citep{imbens2004}. Similar distinctions hold for fixed-horizon uncertainty regions produced by sensitivity-analysis-based models for data with unobserved outcomes \citep{vansteelandt2006}. Work on confidence interval projection for such settings include \citet{kaido2019}, and \citet{dalal2024} who construct confidence intervals over time-varying causal estimands in partially-identified models. TrajCert constructs projections of confidence sequences on resolved endpoints to characterise the entire sensitivity-compatible class of parameters, based on an upper-confidence bound on the free mass term. Categorical (anytime) confidence sequences are obtained coordinate-wise over categories; as an alternative, the construction of \citet{lindon2022} produces simultaneous bounds across the categorical distribution. Anytime validity of the intervals follows from these results \citep{howard2021,ramdas2023}; it is also noted that betting strategies can be used to generate such intervals on restricted-value observations \citep{waudby2024} and general frameworks for sequentially-valid inference over time have been established \citep{johari2022}. Accounting for time-dependence and ordering of observed vs.\ censored outcomes is considered in \citep{lindon2026} in a different causal context. TrajCert assumes an iid collection of events with an equal resolution deadline before admission into the observed set, and considers a stable population risk. Time-varying confidence sets on the projected distribution space follow from conservative application of existing theory and an outer-optimisation step.
